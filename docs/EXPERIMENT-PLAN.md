## Three different kinds of evidence

| Method | What it can establish | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synthetic audit | Reproducible observations about configured actions and rendered states, supported by evidence IDs; candidate friction and regression checks. | Real owner opinions, natural discoverability, population success rates, conversion lift, or accessibility conformance. The engineering rubric is not a human-research score. |
| Research with consenting HVAC owners | Observed comprehension, workarounds, recovery, and task completion in an approved test setting. Capture participants' actual words only with consent. | A small convenience sample does not establish population percentages or the causal effect of shipping a variant. |
| Preregistered randomized comparison | An estimated effect for a defined eligible population, metric, exposure period, and assignment design, with uncertainty. | A guaranteed uplift, proof of every workflow, or an explanation of why an effect occurred. |

Start with a small moderated pilot across different company sizes, setup experience, device habits, and accessibility needs. The five synthetic personas guide recruitment questions; age is not a proxy for ability. Use fictional business data in isolated staging. Give outcome-based tasks without naming interface controls; record assistance separately. Counterbalance task order where appropriate, and do not treat repeated tasks by one participant as independent people. Pilot findings are descriptive and inform revisions before a confirmatory experiment.

## Manual validation before experiments

Perform full keyboard journeys, visible/unobscured focus, modal entry/exit and focus return, screen-reader labels/status changes, error identification, and recovery. Check 320 CSS-pixel reflow, zoom, and user text-spacing overrides across relevant states; exceptions require review, and the absence of page overflow alone is insufficient. W3C defines reflow expectations and the specific text-spacing settings; these are tests of content resilience, not a request to replace brand styles. [W3C Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html), [W3C Text Spacing](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html), [W3C Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html).

Ask owners to explain the setup assistance included, who approves prices, whether a saved draft changes live quotes, and what happens next. Compare their answers with verified product authority. Review labels, grouping, and state feedback using blueprint pp. 1–5 and 7–13. Check cross-page brand consistency against existing tokens, not the blueprint's suggested colors or arbitrary visual quotas (pp. 5–9). Verify every trust claim and every delivery-status description from system evidence (pp. 8–13). A success-looking screen is not proof of persistent storage, publication, provider delivery, or inbox receipt.

## Preregistration template

Complete and approve every field before assigning participants. Save a dated, immutable protocol and variant screenshots. Baseline means an observed rate from the same eligibility, denominator, measurement, and outcome window, not the synthetic audit score.

| Field | Record before exposure |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis and change | One falsifiable hypothesis; control version; one coherent variation; causal mechanism; unchanged scope and brand tokens. |
| Eligibility and baseline | Target cohort, exclusions set before assignment, index business, baseline numerator/denominator, observation dates, missingness, and uncertainty. No selection based on later success. |
| Primary outcome | Exactly one primary metric, event/state definitions, denominator, time origin, completion window, and independent success verification. |
| Assignment and analysis unit | Default: randomize the shared owner/business group; coworkers and other businesses under that owner receive the same variant. Nominate one index business before assignment and record one primary outcome per group. Avoid mixed exposure. Clicks, sessions, tasks, and repeated saves are not independent sample units. |
| Exposure and instrumentation | Stable 50/50 assignment, eligible first-exposure rule, version/assignment logs, outcome window, duplicate-event handling, privacy controls, and verification that control/variant logging agree. Analyze all assigned eligible groups under the chosen assignment rule; report non-exposure and missing data. |
| Effect and precision | Absolute minimum detectable effect (MDE), baseline rate, two-sided alpha 0.05, and planned power 80%. These are proposed defaults to freeze before assignment, not a guarantee of a useful result. |
| Guardrails | Explicit acceptable bounds for errors, lost/incorrect values, support burden, duration, misleading state interpretation, and reliable receipt evidence; define critical safety stops separately. |
| Sample and schedule | Required independent groups per arm from baseline and MDE, allocation, attrition, clustering, and test design; fixed recruitment cap/date and a full outcome window after final enrollment. |
| Analysis and decision | Prespecified difference in primary rates with an interval, test appropriate to the design, missing-data treatment, guardrail method, and an approve/revise/inconclusive decision rule. Record all deviations. |

Sample size must come from the baseline, minimum useful effect, alpha, power, and actual design. A two-proportion planning calculation may suit a single binary outcome from independent groups; clustered/repeated measures or unequal allocation require the corresponding method. Check sensitivity across the plausible baseline range and account for attrition. If feasible traffic cannot support the design, use descriptive usability research and report uncertainty; do not claim a randomized win. The blueprint's universal “100 conversions per variant” rule (pp. 13–14) is not adopted. [NIST sample-size planning](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc242.htm) explains the dependence on effect, significance, and power; [NIST difference-of-proportions testing](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/diffprop.htm) describes the independent-sample comparison.

## Four prioritized candidate experiments

All control baselines below are unknown until measured. The proposed absolute MDEs are planning choices, not expected uplifts. Reconcile each with the observed baseline and ceiling before preregistration; if infeasible, revise the question or retain descriptive research before any random assignment. Use the alpha/power and owner-group rules above. No experiment is active.

| 1. Inject faults only in the approved fixture/staging environment; verify data after reopening. | pp. 1–5, 11–12 |
| 4. Clear enquiry/status language improves correct follow-up. | Current enquiry and delivery labels versus plain HVAC task wording that distinguishes queued, accepted, delivered, failed, and unknown states where the system actually supports them. | Proportion finding the correct fictional enquiry and selecting the appropriate next action without assistance within 3 minutes. Proposed MDE: +8 percentage points. | Wrong-enquiry actions, duplicate follow-up, false belief of homeowner receipt, missing routing evidence, and support requests. ## Stopping, analysis, and release decision

Use a fixed-horizon design: recruit the preregistered sample or reach the fixed cap/date, then wait for every primary outcome window to mature. Do not stop when a favorable p-value appears or extend an inconclusive run opportunistically. If a sequential design is necessary, specify a valid method and boundaries before exposure; do not substitute repeated ordinary p-values. A p-value below 0.05 is not a 95% probability that the variant is better. Report absolute effects, uncertainty, denominators, missingness, protocol deviations, and practical relevance.

Monitor safety, instrumentation, exposure imbalance, and sample-ratio mismatch without treating interim efficacy trends as a launch signal. Pause for data loss, incorrect live prices, unauthorized messages, privacy failures, or misleading receipt claims; document the stop and do not salvage a favorable efficacy claim from a contaminated run. Investigate logging or allocation failures before interpreting results.

Run one confirmatory primary comparison at a time unless a joint multiplicity plan is preregistered. Secondary metrics and unplanned segment analyses remain exploratory. With several confirmatory outcomes/comparisons, specify the error-control method before the run. Do not hide null/negative experiments or pool unlike populations to obtain significance. Blueprint pp. 13–15 inspire the validation workflow; their case-study lifts do not set LeadQuote targets.

Consider a variant only when the prespecified primary rule supports a practically useful improvement, uncertainty is acceptable, and guardrails meet their predefined bounds. “No significant harm” alone does not prove guardrail safety; a noninferiority claim needs a planned margin and adequate precision. Inconclusive results stay inconclusive. Product acceptance, provider receipt checks, accessibility review, release approval, and rollout/rollback remain separate requirements. This plan launches nothing.
